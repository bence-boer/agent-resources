# Market Positioning

How to operationalize brand identity into a defensible market position.

## The STP Model

Segmentation → Targeting → Positioning. Enforces disciplined resource allocation toward high-conversion cohorts.

### Segmentation

Divide the broad market into granular layers:

| Variable | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Demographic | Age, income, education, occupation, family size |
| Geographic | Urban/rural, region, climate, market density |
| Psychographic | Lifestyle, values, risk tolerance, personality |
| Behavioral | Purchase history, brand loyalty, usage rate, occasion |

Calculate the Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) — the realistic slice you can capture.

### Targeting

Evaluate each segment against:

- **Size** — large enough to sustain the business
- **Measurability** — differences between cohorts are quantifiable
- **Reachability** — the segment can be addressed through available channels
- **Profitability** — Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is justified by Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

Reject segments that fail any criterion. Concentrate resources on the highest-scoring cohort.

### Positioning

Define the "why" — the unique identity tailored to the target segment's deepest needs.

Use positioning maps to locate gaps relative to competitors. The position must be:

- **Distinct** from competitors
- **Credible** given the brand's capabilities
- **Relevant** to the target segment
- **Sustainable** over time

## Three Positioning Strategies

| Strategy | Focus | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional | Solving a tangible problem better than alternatives | Utility products, B2B software, CPG |
| Symbolic | Conferring status, prestige, or exclusivity | Luxury, premium fashion, high-end electronics |
| Experiential | Prioritizing sensory stimulation and emotional resonance | Hospitality, experiential retail, lifestyle brands |

Choose one primary strategy. Blending all three dilutes the position.

## Ogilvy's Positioning Principles

Timeless rules from David Ogilvy that safeguard against ephemeral trends:

1. Every advertisement is a long-term contribution to brand image — maintain absolute consistency year after year.
2. Marketing must be truthful.
3. Marketing must be helpful.
4. Marketing must be rooted in empirical facts.
5. Marketing must be driven by a "Big Idea."
6. Marketing must deeply respect the consumer's intelligence.

Resist internal pressure to change campaigns prematurely. Consistency compounds.
