# Brand Identity Frameworks

Core structural models for defining what a brand is and how its value is organized.

## The 5Bs of Brand Equity

Brand equity is the accumulated intangible value of owned brand assets. It is sustained by three interdependent components and organized through a portfolio:

| Component | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Brand Relevance | Degree of intersection with current market needs |
| Brand Image | Total cognitive and emotional associations in the consumer's mind |
| Brand Loyalty | Behavioral trust manifesting as recurring revenue |
| Brand Portfolio | Structural organization of multiple brands to maximize equity across verticals |

Brand Equity sits at the center — Relevance, Image, and Loyalty feed it; the Portfolio distributes it.

## Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism

Defines a brand as a living entity refracting its essence into six interrelated facets across two dimensions: sender vs receiver, external vs internal.

| Facet | Axis | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physique | External · Sender | Tangible characteristics: logo, color palette, packaging, product functionality |
| Personality | Internal · Sender | Character and soul: tone of voice, communication style, behavioral traits |
| Culture | Internal · Sender | Core values, principles, ideological foundation; often tied to origin or corporate ethos |
| Relationship | External · Bridge | Social dynamic and transactional conduct between brand and consumer |
| Reflection | External · Receiver | Stereotypical user the brand projects outwardly to society |
| Self-Image | Internal · Receiver | How the consumer perceives themselves when interacting with the brand |

Use the Prism to audit whether identity is coherent: all six facets should reinforce the same core essence. Misalignment between any two facets signals brand confusion.

## Aaker's Four Perspectives

Complement Kapferer by examining the brand from four angles:

1. **Brand as Product** — scope, attributes, quality, uses, users, country of origin
2. **Brand as Organization** — organizational attributes, local vs global orientation
3. **Brand as Person** — personality traits, brand-customer relationship type
4. **Brand as Symbol** — visual imagery, metaphors, brand heritage

A strong identity is coherent across all four. If any perspective contradicts the others, resolve the conflict before proceeding to positioning.

## How to use these together

1. Define the brand's core essence in one sentence.
2. Map the essence onto all six Kapferer facets — identify gaps or contradictions.
3. Validate through Aaker's four perspectives — ensure no perspective undermines the others.
4. Position the result within the 5Bs — confirm relevance, image, and loyalty alignment.
