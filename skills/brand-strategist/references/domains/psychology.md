# Consumer Psychology

How humans actually make purchasing decisions, and the ethical levers that reduce cognitive friction.

## Kahneman's Dual-System Theory

- **System 1** — fast, automatic, heuristic-driven, emotional. Dominates most purchasing decisions.
- **System 2** — slow, deliberate, analytical. Engaged for high-stakes or novel decisions.

Brand strategy should align with System 1 by default: reduce friction, use established familiarity, lead with emotion. Reserve System 2 appeals (data, specs, comparisons) for high-consideration purchases.

## Key Cognitive Biases

| Bias | Mechanism | Brand Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mere Exposure Effect | Repeated encounters build subconscious familiarity → trust | Consistent visual presence across touchpoints increases preference without conscious evaluation |
| Loss Aversion | Pain of losing exceeds pleasure of gaining an equivalent | Frame messaging around what the prospect stands to lose by not acting |
| Anchoring Effect | First number presented disproportionately influences judgment | Set the initial price point or comparison deliberately to frame perceived value |
| Cognitive Dissonance | Post-decision discomfort when behavior conflicts with belief | Reassure buyers post-purchase through confirmation messaging, reviews, and community |

## Cialdini's Seven Principles of Persuasion

| Principle | Mechanism | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reciprocity | Obligation to return favors | Offer genuine value (research, tools, samples) before any commercial ask |
| Commitment & Consistency | Drive to align future behavior with past actions | Secure small micro-conversions (free signup, newsletter) that prime larger commitments |
| Social Proof | Herd behavior as safety heuristic under ambiguity | Display verified reviews, user counts, community endorsements prominently |
| Authority | Deference to recognized expertise | Integrate certifications, expert endorsements, credentials into visual hierarchy |
| Liking | Compliance with entities that mirror our traits | Humanize the brand — staff stories, shared values, relatable tone |
| Scarcity | Limited availability = higher perceived value | Low-stock alerts, countdown timers, limited editions — use honestly |
| Unity | Tribal identity and collective belonging | Invite audience into co-creation; ask for "advice" not "opinions" |

## Application Rules

- Never fabricate scarcity, social proof, or authority signals. Deceptive use destroys trust permanently.
- Layer principles — the most effective messaging combines 2-3 principles simultaneously.
- Match principle selection to the audience's decision stage: awareness favors mere exposure and social proof; consideration favors authority and reciprocity; conversion favors scarcity and commitment.
