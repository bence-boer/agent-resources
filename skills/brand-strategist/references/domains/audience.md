# Audience Paradigms

How to adapt strategy based on whether the target is a consumer, an SMB, or an enterprise.

## B2C vs B2B

| Dimension | B2C | B2B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target | Individual consumers; broad segments | Organizations; targeted industry niches |
| Primary goal | Emotional loyalty, rapid conversion, retention | Institutional trust, long-term partnerships, proven ROI |
| Sales cycle | Short, transactional, often impulse-driven | Protracted, complex, multi-stage evaluation |
| Messaging tone | Emotional, casual, relatable, entertaining | Authoritative, technical, logical, formally structured |
| Content formats | Short-form social, influencer content, interactive ads | Whitepapers, case studies, technical webinars |
| Decision maker | Individual (or household) | Committee of 6-10 stakeholders (DMU) |

## Account-Based Marketing (ABM)

For high-value enterprise targets, invert the traditional funnel:

1. **Identify** specific high-value target accounts (not broad traffic).
2. **Align** marketing and sales to treat each account as a "market of one."
3. **Deliver** hyper-personalized campaigns, bespoke content, and dedicated outreach tailored to that organization's specific challenges.

ABM is resource-intensive upfront but yields higher ROI and larger deal sizes by eliminating spend on unqualified leads. Use ABM when deal size justifies the investment; use traditional lead generation for volume plays.

## The Decision-Making Unit (DMU)

Enterprise purchases are made by groups of humans, not by "businesses." A typical enterprise DMU has 6-10 stakeholders:

| Role | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Initiator | Identifies the problem, conducts preliminary research, sparks the internal conversation |
| User | Interacts with the product daily; evaluates utility, UX, and workflow impact |
| Influencer | Provides expert opinion and technical evaluation; shapes selection criteria |
| Gatekeeper | Controls information flow; manages vendor access, protects executive time |
| Buyer (Economic) | Handles procurement; evaluates cost, negotiates terms, ensures policy compliance |
| Decision Maker | Final veto/approval authority; evaluates strategic alignment and corporate risk |

**Rules:**
- Map content and messaging to specific DMU roles. A single message cannot satisfy all six.
- Initiators and Users respond to problem-awareness and usability content. Influencers need technical depth. Buyers need ROI evidence. Decision Makers need strategic alignment and risk mitigation.
- In B2B campaigns, systematically address each role's objections rather than broadcasting a generic value proposition.
