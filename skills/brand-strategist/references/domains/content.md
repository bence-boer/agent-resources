# Content Execution and Tactical Frameworks

Proven structures for producing persuasive copy, viral content, and high-performing email.

## Tone of Voice

Calibrate brand voice along four dimensions (Nielsen Norman Group):

| Dimension | Spectrum |
| --- | --- |
| Humor | Funny ↔ Serious |
| Formality | Formal ↔ Casual |
| Respectfulness | Respectful ↔ Irreverent |
| Enthusiasm | Enthusiastic ↔ Matter-of-fact |

Plot the brand's exact coordinates on all four axes. This prevents jarring personality shifts across touchpoints and copywriters. Document these coordinates in the brand style guide.

## AIDA Model

Architectural standard for persuasive copy — landing pages, ads, social posts, sales emails:

1. **Attention** — Scroll-stopping hook targeting an urgent pain point. Interrupt the baseline cognitive state.
2. **Interest** — Relatable context using facts, data, or storytelling that proves understanding of the problem.
3. **Desire** — Present the solution using emotional triggers, social proof, and transformational benefits (not features).
4. **Action** — Singular, unambiguous CTA with psychological urgency or scarcity.

Every piece of conversion-oriented content must follow this sequence. Skipping Interest (jumping straight from hook to CTA) destroys credibility.

## STEPPS Framework (Jonah Berger)

Six drivers of social sharing and virality — viral content is engineered, not accidental:

| Principle | Driver |
| --- | --- |
| Social Currency | People share what makes them look intelligent, exclusive, in-the-know |
| Triggers | Content linked to frequent environmental stimuli stays top-of-mind |
| Emotion | High-arousal emotions (awe, anger, excitement) drive sharing; low-arousal (sadness, contentment) suppresses it |
| Public | Visible brand interactions create imitation through social proof |
| Practical Value | Useful, actionable advice ("news you can use") is shared to help others |
| Stories | Information embedded in narrative is remembered and transmitted far more easily than isolated facts |

Design content to activate at least 2-3 STEPPS principles simultaneously.

## Content Marketing Best Practices

- **Psychographic over demographic** — target by values, lifestyle, and pain points, not just age/income.
- **Original research wins** — proprietary reports and data establish dominant authority.
- **Map to editorial calendar** — systematic production, not ad-hoc creation.
- **SEO intent over keyword stuffing** — optimize for what the searcher actually wants to accomplish.
- **Repurpose aggressively** — a whitepaper becomes social carousels, short-form video, webinar topics, email series.
- **Case studies use StoryBrand** — cast the client as the hero achieving measurable ROI, not a dry technical recitation.
- **User-Generated Content (UGC)** — authentic social proof that scales trust without production cost.

## Email Anatomy

Email remains the highest-ROI owned channel. Structure every email with:

### Subject Line (determines open rate)

- **32-60 characters** for mobile optimization
- Start with an action verb
- Limit punctuation (avoid spam triggers)
- Use merge tags for personalization
- Emojis: deploy with extreme caution and only when on-brand

### Email Structure

1. **Envelope** — sender name, subject line, preheader text
2. **Header** — branded, prominently featuring the logo
3. **Body** — scannable, benefit-focused, follows AIDA internally
4. **Footer** — compliant (unsubscribe link, physical address), consistent brand elements

Maintain visual brand consistency across every email. A branded email that looks different from the website erodes trust.
