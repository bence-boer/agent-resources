# Visual Identity and Naming

Engineering brand names, logos, and typographic systems using linguistics and semiotics.

## Phonosemantics (Sound Symbolism)

The raw phonetic structure of a word conveys meaning independent of dictionary definition. Phoneme choice subconsciously primes consumer perception across all cultures.

| Sound Type | Perception | Sector Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plosives and fricatives (j, p, k, sh, v) | Kinetic energy, speed, dependability, aggressive action | Automotive, high-tech, performance |
| Soft, resonant, continuous sounds (m, l, n, long vowels) | Comfort, elegance, relaxation, warmth | Hospitality, luxury, wellness |
| Short, sharp vowels (i, a) | Smallness, precision, agility | Tech startups, tools |
| Long, open vowels (o, oo, ah) | Largeness, openness, grandeur | Premium, lifestyle |

**Rules:**
- Align phonetic character with the brand's chosen archetype and positioning strategy.
- Test name candidates aloud — the spoken experience matters as much as the written form.
- Verify cross-language pronunciation and meaning to avoid unintended associations.

## Logo Geometry and Semiotics

Geometric shapes carry instinctive psychological weight:

| Shape | Semiotic Meaning | Archetypal Alignment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squares and rectangles | Stability, order, reliability, structural efficiency | Ruler, Sage; B2B, finance |
| Circles and ovals | Unity, continuity, inclusivity; makes products feel comfortable | Caregiver, Everyman; community, lifestyle |
| Triangles | Power, direction, momentum, energy | Hero, Explorer; tech, athletics |

- Choose a primary shape language that reinforces the archetype.
- Composite logos should maintain one dominant geometry.
- Visual consistency across all applications is non-negotiable — define exact clear-space rules based on scale.

## Typography System

Fonts are silent tone-of-voice signals:

| Font Category | Projects | Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serif | Tradition, credibility, institutional authority | Editorial, finance, luxury, legal |
| Sans-serif | Modernity, transparency, innovation | Tech, startups, consumer digital |

Build a scalable typographic system with three tiers:

1. **Primary (heading)** — Attention-grabbing, expressive, carries brand personality.
2. **Secondary (body)** — Highly legible, prevents reading fatigue, neutral enough for long-form.
3. **Accent (UI/callouts)** — Distinct, used sparingly for emphasis and interactive elements.

## Color

Color carries cultural and psychological weight. Define exact values across systems:

- **Pantone** — print production
- **CMYK** — four-color process printing
- **Hex/RGB** — digital screens

Maintain strict contrast ratios for accessibility. The primary palette should be immediately recognizable in isolation — if the logo is removed, color alone should suggest the brand.

## Integration Checklist

1. Name phonetics align with archetype and positioning.
2. Logo geometry reinforces the same archetype.
3. Typography tier matches the brand's formality and modernity axis.
4. Color palette is defined in all required systems with accessibility verified.
5. All elements tested together form a coherent, unified identity.
