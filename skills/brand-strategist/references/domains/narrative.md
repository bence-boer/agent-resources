# Narrative Architecture and Brand Archetypes

How to anchor a brand in universal human psychology and communicate its purpose through story.

## Jungian Brand Archetypes

Archetypes map a brand to primitive, universally recognized desires in the collective unconscious. Selecting one archetype makes the brand intuitively recognizable without explanatory copy.

| Archetype | Core Desire | Behavioral Traits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Innocent | Safety, purity, simplicity | Optimistic, honest, seeks to spread happiness |
| Everyman | Belonging, connection, equality | Relatable, supportive, unpretentious, faithful |
| Hero | Mastery, courage, validation | Bold, inspirational, action-oriented, overcomes adversity |
| Outlaw | Liberation, disruption | Questions authority, breaks rules, drives revolution |
| Explorer | Freedom, discovery, autonomy | Adventurous, independent, pioneering |
| Creator | Innovation, originality, self-expression | Imaginative, inventive, builds things of enduring value |
| Ruler | Control, stability, power | Authoritative, commanding, creates order from chaos |
| Magician | Transformation, mystique | Visionary, charismatic, makes dreams tangible |
| Lover | Intimacy, pleasure, attraction | Passionate, aesthetic, sensory, committed |
| Caregiver | Service, protection, altruism | Compassionate, nurturing, generous, shielding |
| Jester | Enjoyment, humor, living in the moment | Playful, irreverent, mischievous, brings joy |
| Sage | Understanding, wisdom, truth | Analytical, objective, mentoring, insightful |

**Rules:**
- Choose one primary archetype. A secondary archetype is acceptable only when it does not contradict the primary.
- The archetype must be consistent with Kapferer's Personality facet.
- Every brand touchpoint — copy, visuals, tone, behavior — must express the archetype consistently.

## Sinek's Golden Circle

Inspired organizations communicate from the inside out:

1. **Why** (innermost) — The fundamental belief or cause behind the brand's existence. Transcends profit.
2. **How** — The unique values or methodologies that differentiate the brand.
3. **What** (outermost) — The tangible products or services sold.

Communicating Why and How appeals to the limbic system (trust, decisions). Communicating only What appeals to the neocortex (analysis) and fails to generate irrational loyalty.

**Always lead with Why.** If the brand cannot articulate its Why, define it before proceeding to any messaging work.

## StoryBrand Framework (Donald Miller)

The customer is the hero. The brand is the guide.

Seven-part narrative arc:

1. **Character** — The customer, with a specific want or need.
2. **Problem** — Broken into three layers:
   - External: the tangible surface problem
   - Internal: the emotional frustration
   - Philosophical: the deeper injustice ("it shouldn't be this way")
3. **Guide** — The brand, demonstrating empathy ("we understand") and authority ("we can help").
4. **Plan** — A clear, risk-mitigating path forward (typically 3 steps).
5. **Call to Action** — A direct challenge to act. Must be specific and unambiguous.
6. **Success** — Vivid picture of life after the transformation.
7. **Failure** — What happens if they do not act. Establishes narrative stakes.

**Rules:**
- Never position the brand as the hero. Brands that cast themselves as heroes compete with the customer's self-image.
- The Plan must reduce perceived risk. Complexity kills conversion.
- Both Success and Failure must be concrete and emotionally resonant, not abstract.
