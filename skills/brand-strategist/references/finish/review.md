# Brand Review, Governance, and Launch

Final validation: architecture, governance, auditing, and launch execution.

## Brand Architecture Models

How to structure a portfolio when the organization operates multiple brands:

| Model | Structure | Advantages | Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Branded House | Single master brand across all products (Google Maps, FedEx Ground) | Maximum efficiency; unified reach; instant trust transfer to new products | High risk: damage to one product infects the entire portfolio |
| House of Brands | Independent brands under an invisible parent (P&G, Unilever) | Low risk; isolated impact; precise targeting of diverse segments | Massive investment; limited cross-brand efficiency |
| Hybrid | Mix of master extensions and independent entities (Coca-Cola, Salesforce) | Leverages parent equity where useful; protects niche brands | Confusing governance; potential brand cannibalization |
| Endorsed Brand | Independent brands backed by parent credibility (Courtyard by Marriott) | Balances independent personality with institutional trust | Requires careful hierarchy management |

Choose the model before launching additional brands. Retrofitting architecture is expensive and disruptive.

## Brand Style Guide Essentials

The non-negotiable centralized asset that prevents brand erosion:

- **Logo usage** — exact clear-space rules based on scale, minimum sizes, forbidden modifications
- **Color codes** — Pantone, CMYK, Hex for every brand color
- **Typography hierarchy** — primary, secondary, accent fonts with sizing and weight rules
- **Imagery style** — photography direction, illustration style, iconography system
- **Tone of voice** — the four-axis coordinates plus example copy at each register
- **Do/Don't examples** — concrete visual and verbal violations

For decentralized or franchise operations, provide cloud-based access. Unauthorized deviations erode equity compounding over time.

## 10-Step Brand Audit

Regular evaluation to prevent relevance decay:

| Phase | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Prepare | Define audit objectives, secure resources, align stakeholders |
| 2. Gather internal data | Assess employee brand understanding, culture adoption, asset utilization |
| 3. Review collateral | Inventory all visual and textual assets across departments for off-brand materials |
| 4. Competitive analysis | Map positioning against direct and indirect competitors; identify gaps and threats |
| 5. Audience surveys | Measure brand recall, preference, loyalty, and perception quantitatively |
| 6. Analyze consistency | Score departments on adherence to visual and messaging guidelines across channels |
| 7. Assess visibility | Evaluate Share of Search, Share of Voice, and digital presence vs competitors |
| 8. Evaluate reputation | Sentiment analysis on review platforms and social channels |
| 9. Report findings | Executive summaries (risks) + operational reports (specific metric failures) |
| 10. Action plan | Phased roadmap to recalibrate messaging, retrain staff, or update identities based on data |

## Launch Checklist

### Pre-Launch

- [ ] SOM validated
- [ ] Core USP defined
- [ ] KPIs established
- [ ] Budget allocated
- [ ] StoryBrand messaging framework finalized
- [ ] Go-To-Market (GTM) strategy formulated

### Internal Alignment

- [ ] New brand presented to all staff
- [ ] Email signatures updated
- [ ] Style guide distributed and accessible

### Creative Setup

- [ ] Landing pages designed (minimize form fields to reduce friction)
- [ ] CRM and email automation configured
- [ ] All assets QA-tested for cross-platform mobile responsiveness

### Execution

- [ ] Content distributed via strategic channels
- [ ] Social media leveraged for anticipation (progressive reveals)
- [ ] Digital ads launched with verified UTM parameters for attribution
- [ ] Performance data collection active for iterative optimization

## Final Validation Questions

Before delivering any brand strategy work, verify:

1. Is the identity coherent across Kapferer's six facets?
2. Does the positioning pass all four STP targeting criteria?
3. Is there one clear archetype consistently expressed?
4. Does the Golden Circle Why exist and lead all messaging?
5. Is the customer positioned as the hero (not the brand)?
6. Are visual and verbal elements mutually reinforcing?
7. Does the strategy account for the actual audience type (B2C/B2B/ABM)?
8. Is every claim backed by evidence, not aspiration?
