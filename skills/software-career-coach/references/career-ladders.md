# Career Ladders and Scope Calibration

Read this file when the user wants to break into software engineering, move from one engineering level to the next, compare IC versus leadership readiness, or understand how breadth changes promotability.

## Entry Path and First-Role Proof

- Treat formal education as a supporting signal, not a gate. Skills-based hiring is common enough that strong proof of work can outweigh missing degrees.
- For first-role candidates, the strongest evidence is internships, shipped projects, open-source work, freelance or client delivery, and repositories that show clean version control, setup, testing, and problem framing.
- Target one entry path at a time. "Backend engineer," "frontend engineer," or "data engineer" is stronger than presenting every stack touched.
- Judge first-role readiness by whether the user can learn team tooling quickly, ship bounded changes, absorb feedback, and close loops reliably.

## Level Progression Model

| Stage | Dominant expectation | Strong evidence | Common plateau |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry / Junior | Reliable execution on bounded tasks | ships small changes, learns team tooling, asks good questions, closes feedback loops | optimizing for syntax instead of delivery habits |
| Mid-level | Independent feature delivery | owns end-to-end features, handles review feedback well, understands adjacent system boundaries | waits for direction on ambiguous work |
| Senior | System ownership and judgment | owns an important system, mentors peers, makes sound trade-offs, ties work to business goals | remains a strong implementer without cross-team leverage |
| Staff | Force multiplier across teams | initiates high-value work, aligns multiple stakeholders, improves architecture or process beyond one team | keeps acting as the best individual contributor on one backlog |
| Principal and above | Organization or company-scale technical direction | shapes strategy, creates durable technical standards, influences funding and sequencing | confuses visibility with sustained organizational change |

## Track Split

Use the table above for IC progression and technical leadership without direct reports.
Use the ladder below when the user is on a people-management path.

| Leadership stage | Dominant expectation | Strong evidence | Common plateau |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EM | healthy team execution through delegation and feedback | stable planning cadence, hiring participation, team health, fewer escalations routed only through the manager | keeps solving work personally instead of scaling through others |
| Senior EM / Manager-of-Managers | multi-team leverage through managers and planning systems | manager development, headcount planning, better cross-team execution, clearer interfaces | still operates like a single-team EM |
| Director+ / Head of Engineering | org design, staffing strategy, and portfolio trade-offs | budget or staffing influence, sequencing across teams, stronger organizational operating model | optimizes local teams without shaping the wider system |

## Scope Calibration Lenses

Use these lenses before recommending a promotion strategy:

1. **Scope:** What size problem does the user own today?
2. **Autonomy:** Do they need direction, or do they create it?
3. **Ambiguity:** Can they operate when the path is unclear?
4. **Influence:** Do other engineers change behavior because of their work?
5. **Business alignment:** Can they connect technical work to customer, cost, or risk outcomes?

If the user cannot provide evidence in these categories, they likely need more demonstrated scope before asking for title change.

## Skill Topology Heuristics

| Topology | Meaning | Best use | Risk if overused |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I-shaped | deep specialist in one narrow area | hard technical problems in one domain | weak cross-functional leverage |
| T-shaped | deep in one area, broad working knowledge elsewhere | most senior IC roles | can still plateau without business alignment |
| Pi-shaped | deep in two domains plus broad generalism | Staff roles that bridge systems and organizations | complexity of focus; proof burden is higher |
| M-shaped / comb-shaped | multiple deep domains plus leadership and business breadth | Principal, management, or executive tracks | superficial breadth if the verticals are not real |

Use topology language to explain what breadth the user is missing, not to flatter them.

## Promotion Decision Rules

- **Junior to mid-level:** optimize for autonomy, review fluency, and end-to-end feature ownership.
- **Mid-level to senior:** optimize for system ownership, decision quality, and mentoring evidence.
- **Senior to Staff:** optimize for force multiplication, initiative creation, and cross-team trust.
- **Staff to Principal:** optimize for organization-scale strategy, standards, and compounding technical leverage.
- **Technical leadership without reports:** optimize for technical direction, cross-team adoption, and influence without authority.
- **EM to Senior EM / Manager-of-Managers:** optimize for delegation quality, manager leverage, planning horizon, and hiring or staffing ownership.
- **Senior EM to Director+ / Head of Engineering:** optimize for org design, staffing strategy, and portfolio-level trade-offs.

Years of experience matter less than the size and repeatability of the user's impact.

## Example: Building a Staff path over the next year

**Input:**
"I'm a senior backend engineer. My manager says Staff could be realistic next year, but most of my work is still inside one service. How should I spend the next 12 months?"

**Good response shape:**

```markdown
## Career Diagnosis
**Track / Stage:** IC - senior with some technical-lead exposure
**Primary Objective:** staff pathing plan
**Key Constraint:** current scope is still mostly single-system, so the promotion risk is underpowered cross-team evidence

## Leverage Map
- **Technical depth:** strong system ownership inside one service boundary
- **Organizational impact:** limited cross-team leverage so far
- **Visibility / proof:** no clear Staff-sized packet yet

## Phased Plan
### Immediate
- Identify one recurring cross-team problem and write a concrete proposal to improve it.
- Ask your manager what Staff-level scope and evidence would count in the next cycle.

### Next Cycle
- Lead one initiative that changes another team's behavior, not just your own backlog.
- Build a brag document around adoption, reliability, cost, or enablement outcomes.

### Promotion Window / Interview Window
- Push for promotion only once you can show force multiplication, cross-team trust, and business-aligned impact rather than tenure alone.

## Proof to Gather
- adoption signals from another team or stakeholder group
- measurable reliability, cost, or speed improvements from the initiative
- examples of technical direction that changed decisions without formal authority
```

## Coaching Moves

- If the user is trying to land a first role: build role-specific proof before optimizing for long-range titles.
- If the user is below target scope: give them a scope-acquisition strategy.
- If they have scope but weak visibility: route to [promotion and stakeholder alignment](promotion-and-stakeholder.md).
- If they have visibility but weak breadth: recommend adjacent domain expansion.
- If they want Staff without business fluency: teach them to translate systems into cost, risk, or revenue impact.
