# Compensation Negotiation

Read this file when the user is discussing salary expectations, weighing offers, countering a package, or trying to preserve leverage during a hiring process.

## Negotiation Principles

- Negotiation is a normal business process, not a breach of goodwill.
- Leverage is highest after clear interest and strongest after a written offer.
- Objective market data is stronger than emotion.
- Total compensation matters more than base salary alone.
- Collaborative tone beats aggression unless the user is prepared to walk away.

## Process Phases

| Phase | Risk | Best move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early recruiter screen | premature anchoring | defer exact numbers and ask for level, scope, and package details |
| Active interviews | leaking leverage too cheaply | mention active processes without naming every company or bluffing |
| Written offer | under-asking or optimizing one lever only | counter with market-backed range and explore base, equity, sign-on, and review timing |

## Offer-in-Hand Workflow

1. Confirm the full package in writing: level, base, equity, bonus, review cadence, title, and important benefits.
2. Ask for the decision deadline or request a short extension if the package still has major unknowns.
3. Normalize total compensation and note uncertainties before reacting emotionally.
4. Choose one primary lever and one fallback lever before countering.
5. Counter once, then decide rather than reopening the whole package repeatedly.

## Evidence Hierarchy

- **Strongest:** competing written offer, company-specific band information, or recruiter-confirmed level range
- **Strong:** verified compensation databases and local market guides
- **Weakest:** anecdotes, forum hearsay, and generic internet claims

## Useful Scripts

**Deflecting early salary pressure:**
"I would like to understand the scope, level, and full compensation package before committing to a number. I am confident we can find something market-aligned if the fit is strong."

**Using active processes without sounding adversarial:**
"I am in process with other companies, but I am seriously interested here and willing to discuss timing transparently."

**Countering a written offer:**
"I am excited about the role and the team. Based on the market data I have for this level and scope, I was expecting a higher total package. Is there flexibility on base, equity, or sign-on to close that gap?"

## Total Compensation Levers

| Lever | Typical flexibility | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base salary | low to moderate | often constrained by internal bands |
| Equity / RSUs | moderate to high | inspect grant size, cliff, vesting cadence, and refresher policy |
| Signing bonus | high | useful when bands are rigid |
| Review cadence | moderate | early review can partly offset a conservative entry point |
| Non-monetary terms | variable | remote flexibility, PTO, education budget, title timing |

## Guardrails

- Do not negotiate against yourself by volunteering the minimum you would accept.
- Do not bluff competing offers.
- Do not issue ultimatums unless the user is genuinely willing to walk.
- If geography is important, calibrate locally before recommending a number.
- Stay at compensation-strategy level; do not interpret contracts, equity tax treatment, severance, or employment-law terms. Recommend qualified legal or tax help when those become central.

## Example: Negotiating with local market context

**Input:**
"I have a Budapest offer at 1.55M HUF gross with small equity. I am a senior Python engineer. They want an answer tomorrow. What should I do?"

**Good response shape:**

```markdown
## Career Diagnosis
**Track / Stage:** IC - senior external move
**Primary Objective:** offer evaluation and negotiation
**Key Constraint:** time pressure is being used before market calibration is complete

## Leverage Map
- **Technical depth:** senior backend profile in a scarce skill area
- **Market leverage:** written interest exists, but external alternatives are unclear
- **Visibility / proof:** package details and level calibration are incomplete

## Options and Trade-offs
- **Option A:** accept quickly and remove uncertainty; downside is giving up negotiation leverage and possibly anchoring below market.
- **Option B:** slow the process slightly, calibrate against Budapest senior Python bands, and counter; downside is short-term process friction.

## Recommendation
Do not answer immediately. Ask for the full package in writing, calibrate against local senior Python ranges, and counter collaboratively. If base salary is near the lower end of market and bands are rigid, push on sign-on, review timing, or equity rather than base only.

## Next Moves
1. Ask for the full compensation breakdown and the level/band in writing.
2. Counter with a market-backed range and one fallback lever such as sign-on.
3. Clarify remote expectations, review cadence, and growth path before accepting.

## Proof to Gather
- current Budapest senior Python salary range for similar employers
- actual equity mechanics, bonus terms, and review timing
- whether the role expands scope or only changes pay
```

## Coaching Moves

- If the user has weak leverage: improve evidence or create alternatives before aggressive asks.
- If the user has an offer but unclear package value: break down total comp before advising.
- If internal bands are rigid: pivot to sign-on, equity, remote flexibility, or accelerated review timing.
